# Pricing math

Load in Step 2. All figures below are illustrative. Use the prospect's real numbers.

## Anchor 1: payroll relieved

    loaded cost per role per month = salary per month x (1 + burden)
    burden default 0.25 (taxes, benefits, PTO); use theirs if they know it
    relieved payroll = sum over roles taken over, minus roles they keep to supervise

Example (illustrative): a clinic group runs 4 schedulers at $4,000 a month loaded each = $16,000. The system handles phones and scheduling; they keep 1 scheduler to supervise and handle edge cases. Relieved = 3 x $4,000 = $12,000 a month.

Price check: a monthly of $7,000 to $8,000 leaves them $4,000 to $5,000 a month better off from day one. A monthly of $11,000 leaves them $1,000; that is a fragile deal even if they sign. If you need to be near $11,000, waive or spread the build and add scope they value (more locations, languages, referral intake) so the value side grows.

## Anchor 2: revenue recovered

    recovered per month = missed or slow leads per month x share that would book x close rate after booking x value per customer

Use their own close rate. If unknown, use a conservative assumption and label it.

Example (illustrative): a roofing company misses 40 calls a month after hours. Say 30% would have booked an inspection, 25% of inspections close, average job $12,000. Recovered = 40 x 0.30 x 0.25 x 12,000 = $36,000 a month in sales. At a 25% gross margin that is $9,000 a month in profit. Price against profit, not top-line sales, when you talk to an owner who knows their margins.

## Anchor 3: money back from the back office

Unpaid claims, denied authorizations, patient balances, unsold estimates. Value = amount recovered, timing = slow. Usually priced as a monthly plus a percentage of recovered, or a higher monthly once you have a track record. Do not lead with this until the front-office work has earned trust.

## Build fee

Rough guide: the build fee reflects real setup work. Integration with their scheduling or practice system, scripting, testing on real calls, training staff. A common shape is 1 to 3 months of the monthly fee. Show it as a standard value even if you waive it, so the concession is visible.

## Term and discounts

- Standard rate month to month.
- 12-month agreement: lower monthly or build waived. Frame as a standard agreement discount.
- Multi-location: per-location rate that drops at volume.
- Paid upfront annually: one or two months free.

Never call any of these beta, pilot-only or "first client" pricing in the document.

## Hours blocks for ongoing custom work

Offer two or three blocks (e.g. a smaller and a larger monthly block of hours), an hourly overage rate, unused hours expire monthly, smallest block is the minimum. The client chooses; your price per hour drops a little on the bigger block.

## Sanity checks before sending
- Is every input labeled (they said / estimated)?
- Does the Recommended option leave them clearly better off in year one?
- Would the math survive the buyer's accountant looking at it?
- Is there anything here you would be embarrassed to defend on a call?
