# Compliance for medical, legal and financial ads

Load this whenever the offer is in healthcare, law, finance, insurance, credit or housing. This is practical guidance, not legal advice; when an ad makes a claim you are unsure about, flag it for the client's own counsel or compliance person.

## Meta's own rules (apply to every ad)

- No personal attributes aimed at the viewer: "Are you in debt?", "Struggling with your weight?", "Other people with diabetes..." are rejected. Speak about the situation, not the person's condition: "Debt relief options in Texas", "A clinic for knee pain".
- Special Ad Categories: credit, employment, housing, and social issues, elections or politics must be declared. Targeting is then limited (no age, gender or detailed ZIP targeting). Declare it rather than risk the account.
- Before/after images for health, weight and cosmetic results are restricted or banned. Do not use them.
- No implied knowledge of the viewer's health, finances or legal trouble in the creative or the targeting.

## Medical and healthcare

- No promised outcomes ("cure", "guaranteed results", "pain-free"). Describe the service, the access, the experience.
- Testimonials: real patients, written consent, typical-results disclosure where results vary. No patient identifiers or health details without explicit written authorization (HIPAA applies to the practice's use of patient information in marketing).
- Prices and insurance: only what the practice confirms in writing.
- Remarketing on health topics: avoid building audiences from pages about specific conditions; keep pixels off forms that collect health information unless the setup has been reviewed.
- Physicians named in ads: credentials stated accurately (board certified only if true for that board).

## Legal

- Most US state bars treat ads as attorney advertising: some require the words "Attorney Advertising", a named responsible attorney, or office location. Check the state's rules for the firm.
- No guarantees of outcome, no "we win", no past results without the state's required disclaimer ("Past results do not guarantee a similar outcome") and only if the state allows past results at all.
- "Specialist" or "expert" only where the state allows it and the lawyer holds the certification.
- Dramatizations and actors portraying clients or lawyers usually need a disclosure on screen.
- No solicitation language aimed at people known to be in a specific accident or crisis.

## Financial, debt, credit, insurance

- Declare the credit Special Ad Category for credit offers.
- No guaranteed approval, guaranteed savings or "erase your debt". Results-based claims need typical-results context.
- Debt relief in the US has FTC Telemarketing Sales Rule limits on advance fees; do not write "no fees until results" unless the client confirms that is how they actually charge.

## Calls and texts after the ad

An ad that leads to an AI call or text needs consent captured on the form: clear language that the person agrees to be called and texted, including by automated systems, at the number given. No consent, no AI outreach. (TCPA.)

## AI disclosure

If an ad shows an AI voice or a generated person, do not imply it is a real customer or staff member. A generated character can demonstrate; it cannot give a testimonial.
