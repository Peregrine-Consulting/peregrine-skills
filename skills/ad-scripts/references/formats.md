# Format templates

Load this while writing a script, for the format chosen in Step 3. Each template is a starting beat structure; bend it to the angle.

## Founder or owner to camera (30 to 60s)

- 0-3s: hook, direct to lens, phone at eye level, close crop. Movement in the first second (walking in, sitting down, holding up an object).
- 3-12s: the problem as they have watched customers live it. One specific moment.
- 12-25s: what they do differently and why. One mechanism.
- 25-40s: proof. A real result or a short story with a real customer (consent).
- 40-50s: the action, and what happens after they take it ("you'll get a call back in under a minute").
- Shoot: natural light facing a window, lav or phone mic close, 3 takes. Burned-in captions.

## UGC (20 to 45s)

- 0-3s: creator mid-action or mid-sentence, as if already talking to a friend.
- 3-15s: "I was dealing with X..." (real customer) or "I tried X so you don't have to" (creator demonstrating).
- 15-30s: showing it working, phone in hand, real screen.
- 30-40s: the action, casual.
- Brief the creator with the angle, the 3 must-say points and the forbidden claims. Not a word-for-word script; ask for 3 variations of the first line.

## Green screen (20 to 40s)

- Background: a screenshot of a news headline, a review, a competitor claim, a chart, a text message.
- 0-3s: point at it, react in one line.
- 3-20s: explain what it means for the buyer.
- 20-30s: what to do about it, the action.
- Crop the background so its key line is readable at phone size. Cite the source on screen for news.

## Whiteboard or pen-on-paper (30 to 60s)

- Top-down camera, hands only or presenter beside a board.
- Drawing 1: where the money or time leaks today (a funnel with holes, calls going to voicemail).
- Drawing 2: the fix plugging the holes.
- Drawing 3: the result, in the buyer's unit.
- Speed up the drawing to 2x to 4x; keep the voice at 1x.

## 3D animated character (15 to 40s)

- One character with a clear look that recurs across ads (the stressed receptionist, the phone that never stops ringing). Write a fixed character description and reuse it in every generation prompt so it stays consistent.
- Same beats as founder to camera, but shorter lines. The character can say the uncomfortable thing a real person would not.
- For image or video generation, write per shot: subject, action, camera angle, setting, lighting, mood. Keep text out of generated frames; add on-screen text in the edit.

## Demo screen recording (15 to 45s)

- 0-5s: the result first (the AI answering the call, the booked appointment landing in the calendar).
- 5-25s: how it got there, 2 or 3 steps max, zoomed so it reads on a phone.
- 25-35s: the action. For a voice product, "call this number and try it" with the number on screen is the strongest close there is.

## Live call recording (20 to 60s)

- Waveform or phone screen with captions synced to the audio.
- Pick the 20 seconds where the call sounds most human (a joke, a correction, handling an interruption).
- Consent from everyone recorded; replace any real names, dates of birth, addresses or account details with a fictional demo call if needed.

## Text-on-screen listicle or meme (7 to 15s)

- One relatable line on a trending visual template, then the turn ("unless..."), then the handle or offer.
- Use for testing angles cheaply. The winner gets a proper script.
