# Angle library

Load when picking an angle, or when the user wants to see alternatives. Every example below is illustrative: the business, city and details are made up. Replace every specific with something true about the real lead.

The rule behind all of them: one observation, one problem, one outcome, one call to action. The ask is always small and specific, never the full menu and never a price.

## Missed calls (voice receptionist, answering, speed to lead)

When: phone complaints in reviews, closed evenings or weekends, phone-only booking, a job post for a receptionist.

Core message: the calls you miss are customers who booked with someone else, and you never find out who they were.

Observations that work:
- "Three of your reviews this year mention calls going to voicemail."
- "Your site says to call to book, and the office closes at 5."
- "You're hiring a front desk coordinator."

Example email (illustrative):

    subject: calls after 5 at Desert Ridge

    Hi Dr. Lee,

    I noticed your office closes at 5 and booking is by phone only. Most people looking for a dentist search after work, and the ones who reach voicemail usually call the next practice on the list.

    We set up a receptionist that answers every call, day or night, and books straight into your schedule.

    Worth hearing what it sounds like on your kind of call?

    Sam, Northline

## Reputation

When: rating under 4.2, few or stale reviews, a bad review at the top, an unclaimed profile.

Core message: buyers read reviews before they call, so a weak profile loses the customer before you ever hear from them.

Observations: thin review count next to competitors, no new reviews in months, a one-star review with no owner reply sitting first, an incomplete profile.

The ask: a short reputation and visibility audit.

## AI search

When: you asked an assistant for the best option in their city and competitors were named, they were not.

Core message: more buyers ask ChatGPT or Gemini who to call. If you are not in the answer, a competitor gets the call before you know it existed.

Write it as what you saw, with engine and date: "I asked ChatGPT for the best estate planning attorney in Raleigh this morning. Three firms came up, yours wasn't one of them." Never claim "AI never recommends you" from one or two samples. Position it as one more place buyers look, not a replacement for reviews or SEO.

The ask: a short AI visibility audit, offered with the screenshot.

## Lead flow and ROI

When: they run ads, are hiring or expanding, or have strong reviews but a weak path from click to booked call.

Core message: you are already paying to get leads. The money is in what happens in the first five minutes after one arrives.

Observations: Meta or Google ads running, an ad that lands on a homepage instead of a booking page, a contact form with no instant response, no call tracking on a site that advertises.

Ask the question instead of asserting: "Is the bigger goal right now more leads, or getting more of the ones you already pay for onto the calendar?"

The ask: a 15-minute look at where their paid leads go after the click.

## Consolidation (they already have tools)

When: they already pay for booking software, a CRM, chat widgets, call tracking.

Tools are a buying signal, not a reason to skip. The angle is getting results from what they already pay for, or replacing three tools with one. Never "you need your first system".

## Picking when two angles tie

1. Pain the owner already feels beats pain you have to explain.
2. A signal with a number beats one without.
3. A signal from the last 90 days beats an old one.
4. The angle your proof.md can back with a real result beats one it cannot.
