# Finding the questions worth a page

Load this in Phase 1.

The best questions come from the people who answer the phone. Ask the user first: "What do callers ask most? What do prospects ask on sales calls right before they buy or walk?" Ten minutes with a front-desk lead or a salesperson beats a day of keyword tools.

## Sources, in order of value

1. Call transcripts, sales call notes, intake forms, support inbox, the FAQ the front desk keeps on a sticky note.
2. ./business/customers.md (buyer words, objections, triggers).
3. A previous ai-visibility-audit: every question where the business was absent is a page candidate, and the cited winners show what the page must contain.
4. Reviews (the business's and competitors'): what people praise or complain about is what new buyers ask about.
5. Reddit, Facebook groups, Nextdoor, industry forums: search "[service] [city] reddit", "[problem] who should I call".
6. Google's People Also Ask and autocomplete for the main service plus city.
7. Asking the engines directly: "What questions do people ask before choosing a [service] in [city]?" Use as a prompt for ideas, never as evidence.

## Which questions earn a page

Score each candidate on three things, high / medium / low:

- Money: does someone asking this buy soon? ("Does your clinic take Medicare for vein treatment?" is high. "What is a varicose vein?" is low.)
- Fit: can this business give the best answer on the internet, with facts only it has?
- Gap: did the audit show the engines answering it without the business, or with a weak source?

Write pages for high-high-anything first. A page with no money and no fit is content marketing filler; skip it.

## Clustering

Group questions that would have the same short answer into one page, with the variants as the page's FAQs. One page per distinct answer. Thirty thin pages that each restate the same thing is worse than ten strong ones.

Typical hub for a local service business, 15 to 30 pages:

- Cost and payment (price ranges, insurance or financing, what drives cost)
- Choosing (how to pick a provider, questions to ask, red flags)
- Comparisons (you vs the alternatives the buyer considers: DIY, a bigger chain, a different treatment)
- Situations (emergencies, timelines, specific jobs, specific neighborhoods)
- Process (what happens at the first visit, how long, what to bring)
- Trust (licensing, warranties, privacy, who does the work)
