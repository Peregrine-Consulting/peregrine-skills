# Comparison and "best of" pages: the rules

Load this before writing any page that compares the business (or its product) with competitors, or any "best X for Y" page the business publishes.

Comparison pages are among the most-cited page types in AI answers, because buyers ask "X vs Y" and "best X for Y" all the time. They are also where businesses get into legal and reputation trouble. These rules keep the page citable and defensible.

## Disclosure (FTC material connection)

If the publisher makes, sells or profits from anything the page recommends, say so clearly and near the top. The FTC Endorsement Guides treat an undisclosed connection between a reviewer and the thing reviewed as deceptive.

- A visible notice at the top of every page, near the start of the body, and in the footer. Example: "Published by [Company], maker of [Product], which this guide recommends. This is not an independent review."
- Never call the page "independent", "unbiased" or "third-party" when it is not.
- If a separate site or brand is used for the guide, the disclosure names the real publisher. A separate domain does not remove the connection.
- Put the disclosure in llms.txt and in the publisher Organization schema too, so engines quoting the page carry it.

## Facts about competitors

- Only from the competitor's own public pages, with a link and the date checked. "Not stated" when you cannot verify.
- No ratings, star scores, "user counts" or review quotes about competitors unless you have a real, citable source.
- No prices unless the competitor publishes them. Dated.
- Describe who each competitor fits better. "Better fit for health systems with an IT team" is useful and fair, and a page that only ever recommends the publisher reads as an ad and gets cited less.
- Have a corrections path: an email on the method page, and a dated change log when something is corrected.

## Your own claims

- Only claims you can prove. Each result needs a source the user can produce: a named client who agreed to be named, or an anonymous description that is still true ("a 5-location specialty group").
- No guarantees of outcomes in regulated fields (medical, legal, financial). State what the product or service does, not what it will achieve.
- "Best" in a page title is fine as the buyer's question ("What is the best...?"). As a factual claim about yourself it needs support.

## Structure that gets cited

1. Title is the buyer's question, verbatim.
2. Short answer first: a two- or three-sentence verdict that names who each option fits. This is the sentence engines lift.
3. A comparison table with plain-language cells and the wording each mark is based on.
4. "Who should choose what" section, one paragraph per option.
5. How we compared (method), date, author, disclosure.
6. Sources with dates.
