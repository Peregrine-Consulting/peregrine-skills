# Using the audit as a lead magnet

Load this when the business being audited is a prospect, not the user's own business or a paying client.

The audit is one of the best door-openers an AI services seller has. It is specific, about the prospect's own business, names the competitors beating them, and costs a few dollars to run. A prospect who opens a report naming three rivals that ChatGPT recommends instead of them reads the whole thing.

## The quick version for outreach

For cold prospects, run a light audit: 3 to 5 provider-seeking questions, two or three engines, the site signals scan, no reputation research. Score it the same way (the method line will show what was not checked). Save the full audit for after they reply.

Pick the questions the prospect would most hate to lose: their main service plus their city.

## Hard rules

- Every claim in the report and message comes from captured answers. If ChatGPT named them, say so. Never imply worse results than you observed.
- Never send, post or upload anything without the user's explicit OK on the final text.
- Cold email only to business addresses, with the user's real name, a physical address and an opt-out line (CAN-SPAM). No AI calls or texts to cold prospects without consent (TCPA). Email first; calls and texts only after they opt in.
- Host the report somewhere the prospect can open without logging in, with noindex. A PDF attachment hurts deliverability on a cold first touch; send a link.

## Email (4 to 6 sentences, plain text)

Subject: short, specific, no clickbait. "[City] [service] and ChatGPT" or "who ChatGPT recommends for [service] in [city]".

Body pattern:
1. What you checked, in one line: "I asked ChatGPT, Gemini and Perplexity who the best [service] in [city] is."
2. The specific result, quoting one real answer: "ChatGPT named [Competitor A] and [Competitor B]. [Business] did not come up in any of the 9 answers."
3. The one reason, from the audit: "The firms it picked all have a page that answers [question] directly; yours does not yet."
4. The report link.
5. A low-friction ask: "Worth a 15-minute call to walk through the three fixes?"

## LinkedIn DM (casual, 4 short paragraphs)

Hey [first name],
I ran a quick check on who AI assistants recommend for [service] in [city].
[Business] came up in [x] of [y] answers. [Competitor] came up in [z]. Report here: [link]
Happy to walk you through the top fix if useful. No pitch deck.

## Follow-up

Day 3: one new fact from the audit ("Perplexity cites [directory] for every answer, and you are not listed there.").
Day 7: short close ("Should I stop sending these?").
Re-run the same frozen questions in 30 days: a changed result is the best reason to reach out again.
